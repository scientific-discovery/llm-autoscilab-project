Abstract

Scientific discovery is a closed-loop process where hypotheses guide data acquisition and observations refine the hypothesis space. Yet most approaches reduce discovery to supervised learning over fixed datasets, where limited observations can support multiple plausible mechanisms that fit locally but fail to generalize. Thus, the key challenge is selecting informative observations to resolve uncertainty, shifting the focus from static inference to adaptive data acquisition. To address this, we propose LLM-AutoSciLab, a closed-loop framework that couples hypothesis generation with hypothesis-conditioned experiment selection and mechanism refinement. Rather than fitting models to passively collected data, LLM-AutoSciLab iteratively proposes plausible hypotheses, selects informative experiments to distinguish or refine them, and updates its state using the resulting evidence. To evaluate dynamic, closed-loop scientific discovery with active data acquisition, we introduce ActiveSciBench, comprising two datasets: (i) ActiveSciBench-Chem (57 enzyme-kinetics tasks) and (ii) ActiveSciBench-GRN (45 gene-regulatory-network tasks), that model discovery as a budget-constrained process requiring adaptive experiment design, variable selection, and recovery of true mechanisms. Across NewtonBench, ActiveSciBench-Chem, and ActiveSciBench-GRN, LLM-AutoSciLab outperforms prior methods, achieving 67.6% and 35.1% symbolic accuracy on NewtonBench and ActiveSciBench-Chem, respectively, and 31.1% exact graph recovery on ActiveSciBench-GRN. Moreover, hypothesis-guided experimentation is 2–5× more sample-efficient than the strongest competing baselines.

Method Overview

LLM-AutoSciLab treats discovery as active, hypothesis-conditioned experiment design. The LLMs define a space of competing mechanisms and where they disagree; experiments are chosen to separate, refine or falsify them, and the evidence flows back into memory.

A

Hypothesis generation

A small LLM (Qwen2.5-7B) is sampled in batches and its proposals are grouped into mechanism families until the distribution stabilizes. A larger LLM (GPT-4o-mini) turns that set into a primary hypothesis, alternatives, and diagnostic search regions.

B

Hypothesis-conditioned experiment selection

While confidence is low, the next experiment goes where competing mechanisms predict most differently inside the LLM-proposed regions (disambiguate). Once a mechanism is stable, experiments improve its fit (refine).

C

Optimization and confidence feedback

Hypotheses are refit on the new data (PySR or direct skeleton fits for equations, BFGS for graphs). A bootstrap gate refits on resampled data: stable mechanisms gain confidence, brittle ones are flagged, and the verdict is written to memory.

ActiveSciBench

Existing benchmarks hand the learner a fixed dataset. ActiveSciBench gives it a hidden experimental system, a query budget, and inputs of which only some matter: the learner has to design experiments, find the relevant variables, and recover the true mechanism.

57

ActiveSciBench-Chem

Enzyme-catalyzed reactions with a hidden rate law. Every experiment sets a shared 7-D assay interface (substrate, inhibitor, second substrate, product, enzyme, temperature, pH) and returns the initial rate. Tiers run from textbook families to cooperative and allosteric kinetics.

45

ActiveSciBench-GRN

Hidden regulatory systems built from 5 motif families × 3 topological variants × 3 difficulty levels. Experiments knock nodes up or down and observe every downstream change; the target is the signed directed graph, among 320 ≈ 3.5×109 possibilities.

12

NewtonBench

Counterfactual physics laws across 12 domains, from gravitation and Snell’s law to the Bose–Einstein distribution. Variables are known, so it isolates active symbolic law discovery under a query budget.

Fitting the data is not finding the law

Exact accuracy only asks whether predictions match the oracle; symbolic accuracy asks whether the recovered expression is the true mechanism. Fit-driven baselines land far below the diagonal: PySR and Bayesian optimization match NewtonBench data on about 70% of tasks but recover the law on only 24%.

Qualitative Results

What the recovered mechanisms look like, for one symbolic law and one regulatory graph.

A NewtonBench law with two components

The hidden law adds a restoring term and a nonlinear damping term. Only LLM-AutoSciLab recovers both, with a fitted coefficient of 0.4915 ≈ 1/2 and a damping exponent within about 1% of the truth.

Fit-driven baselines add spurious terms

PySR inserts a sinusoidal component and Bayesian optimization finds an entangled numerical fit: low local error, wrong mechanism.

LLM baselines collapse to textbook forms

LLM-only and code-assisted LLMs return a simplified harmonic law and miss the nonlinear damping entirely.

Experiment design alone stops short

Bayesian experiment design moves toward the right components but still converges to a fit-driven approximation.

Robustness to Observation Noise

Gaussian noise injected at increasing levels. Bars are exact accuracy; lines are RMSLE (NewtonBench, Chem) or edge F1 (GRN). Symbolic recovery degrades in steps while errors rise smoothly, and on GRN noise mainly breaks exact topology while edge-level structure largely survives.
